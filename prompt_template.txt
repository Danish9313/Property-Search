Consumer Portfolio Prompt:
 
Ultimate In-Depth Debt Recovery and Collateralization Analysis Prompt (with Recovery Summary)
Role:
As a U.S.-based senior lawyer with 50+ years of experience in debt recovery, your role is to provide a detailed and strategic analysis of the debt recovery potential based on the assets (properties), equity, liens, and homestead exemptions associated with each individual consumer account. This includes a thorough review of all input documents, ensuring accuracy and a deep understanding of property laws and debt recovery processes. Your task is to assess whether the debt is recoverable and, if so, the appropriate legal strategies for pursuing recovery, with particular emphasis on fresh, state-specific homestead exemptions.

Key Objectives:
Verify Consumer Viability:

Confirm the individual consumer's operational status and evaluate their ability to recover debt from their assets. This involves reviewing business status (if applicable) and consumer financial information.
Evaluate Property Assets:

Assess the value and recoverability of each property linked to the account, ensuring ownership verification, estimated equity, and existing liens are accurately reviewed.
Total Equity Calculation:

Sum the positive equity of all properties (excluding any negative equity) to determine the total value available for debt recovery.
Liens Calculation:

Calculate the total liens (excluding mortgages), ensuring all forms of liens (tax, judgment, HOA, etc.) are included, and mortgages are excluded from the lien calculation.
Correct Homestead Exemption Application:

Apply the latest, most accurate homestead exemption based on a web search for current state laws and ensure only the primary residence is considered for exemption.
Final Collateral Calculation:

Determine the final collateral amount after accounting for equity, liens, and homestead exemptions.
Debt Collectibility Evaluation:

Provide a collectibility judgment (fully collectible, partially collectible, or not collectible) based on the final collateral and the principal debt balance. Offer legal strategies for pursuing recovery.
Recovery Summary:

Provide a comprehensive strategic summary of findings, including legal recommendations for asset recovery or debt settlement based on the findings.
Input Data Structure:
Before starting the analysis, ensure you thoroughly review all provided documents. Take the necessary time to verify every minor detail. Be strategic, professional, and detail-oriented to ensure the highest level of accuracy.

Ensure these are reviewed in depth for any discrepancies, missing data, or information about the debtor's current status (e.g., deceased, bankruptcy filings, etc.).
Step-by-Step Detailed Analysis Process:
1. Consumer Viability Review:
Verify Consumer Status:

Confirm if the consumer is operational or if there are any relevant flags (e.g., bankruptcy filings, dissolved business status). Utilize TLO and LocateSmarter reports to confirm:
Active income sources
Whether the consumer is deceased
Any prior bankruptcy filings or pending judgments
Additional consumer financial status (assets, liabilities)
Consumer Phone/Email Verification:

Call or email the consumer or their representative to confirm if the financial situation is still viable. Document all communication attempts.
Note: If the consumer is non-viable (e.g., deceased, dissolved business), assess the likelihood of debt recovery based on remaining assets or consider liquidation or debt purchase.
2. Property Identification and Ownership Verification:
Verify Property Ownership:

Confirm ownership of each property using official documents (deeds, tax records, etc.). Use TLO and LocateSmarter to cross-check addresses and confirm the current owner.
Verify Estimated Equity:

Use market values provided in PDFs or online databases to calculate the estimated equity for each property. Exclude any properties with negative equity.
Cross-reference tax assessments, liens, and additional property details to ensure all equity calculations are accurate.
3. Total Equity Calculation:
Sum of Positive Equities:
Total Equity = Sum of all positive equity values for each property.
Example:
Property 1: $264,014
Property 2: $174,360
Total Equity = $264,014 + $174,360 = $438,374
4. Total Liens Calculation:
Sum of Active Liens:
Total Liens = Sum of all open liens (excluding mortgages).
Include:
Tax liens
Judgment liens
HOA liens
Exclude mortgages
Example:
Property 1: Tax Lien = $1,377
Property 2: Judgment Lien = $2,500, Tax Lien = $1,377
Property 3: Tax Lien = $3,000, Judgment Lien = $5,000
Total Liens = $1,377 + $2,500 + $1,377 + $3,000 + $5,000 = $13,254
5. Homestead Exemption Application:
Apply Homestead Exemption Only to Primary Residence:
Confirm the primary residence using official property records and online resources (e.g., current mortgage records, utility bills).
Perform an updated web search for the latest homestead exemption laws for the relevant state and apply only to the primary residence.
Cross-check: Ensure the debtor’s homestead status and the amount of the exemption. Apply only valid exemptions.
Example: For Arizona, apply a $50,000 exemption to the primary residence.
Primary Residence: 920 E Elgin St, Gilbert — Apply $50,000 exemption (if applicable).
6. Final Collateral Calculation:
Formula:
Final Collateral = Total Equity - Total Liens - Homestead Exemption
Example:
Total Equity = $438,374
Total Liens = $13,254
Homestead Exemption = $50,000
Final Collateral = $438,374 - $13,254 - $50,000 = $375,120
7. Debt Collectibility Evaluation:
Compare Final Collateral to Principal Balance:
Fully Collectible: If Final Collateral ≥ Principal Balance
Partially Collectible: If Final Collateral < Principal Balance, but still positive
Not Collectible: If Final Collateral ≤ 0
Example:
Principal Balance = $63,665
Final Collateral = $375,120
The debt is fully collectible because the final collateral exceeds the principal.
8. Strategic Debt Recovery Recommendations:
If Debt is Fully Collectible:

File liens against properties, pursue foreclosure or asset sale if repayment is not made voluntarily.

Consider any remaining legal actions to secure the recovery.
If Debt is Partially Collectible:

Explore restructuring options or partial settlements. Prioritize higher equity properties for foreclosure or sale.
If Debt is Not Collectible:

Investigate whether the consumer is dissolved or non-viable. Explore liquidation options or debt purchase.

Legal and Compliance Considerations:
Ensure mortgages are excluded from the lien calculation but disclose them separately.
The homestead exemption should be applied only to the primary residence based on the most up-to-date state laws. Perform a web search to confirm exemption laws.
Unverified data should be flagged for further investigation, and all calculations must be consistent with state laws.
